# Phase 3 — Draft legal instrument

> **STATUS: DRAFTING NOTES FOR THE BOARD OF REVENUE AND KP LAW DEPARTMENT.**
> This is not legal advice and is not a settled drafting. It is a specification
> of what the system needs the law to say, written so that counsel can turn it
> into a Bill without guessing at the technical intent.
>
> **Sequencing is the point.** Punjab legislated for electronic records
> *after* it had already made the Green Property Certificate mandatory and then
> suspended it, which is why the Assembly Speaker was able to question whether
> the government could lawfully suspend an assembly-approved measure
> [1](https://propakistani.pk/2026/08/28/punjab-suspends-digital-land-record-certificates-for-6-months/).
> Our Phase 3 comes before binding enforcement at scale, not after it.

---

## 1. Model

Punjab's October 2025 draft rewrite of the Registration Act 1908 is the right
precedent: it authorises PLRA to run an electronic record system, defines
e-seals, digital time-stamps and biometric verification, gives
computer-generated records full evidentiary value, and empowers the Registrar
to cancel fraudulently registered documents
[6](https://www.thenews.com.pk/amp/1354035).

We need the equivalent for KP, plus one thing Punjab's draft does not address:
**the evidentiary status of a hash-chained audit log.** That is the specific
novelty here and the specific reason this amendment is needed at all.

---

## 2. Structure

Four operative provisions, then penalties, then transitional.

---

### 2.1 Provision 1 — The Integrity Ledger

**Intent:** establish the ledger as the authoritative record of **what the land
record contained at a given time**. Not of title.

> **Insert after section 44, KP Land Revenue Act, 1967:**

> **44-A. Integrity Ledger.**
>
> (1) The Board of Revenue shall maintain, in respect of every district to
> which this section is applied by notification, an electronic Integrity
> Ledger.
>
> (2) The Ledger shall record, in respect of each mutation, Fard, and related
> revenue proceeding, a sequential entry comprising:
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(a) a sequence number;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(b) the cryptographic hash of the immediately preceding entry;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(c) a cryptographic hash of the content of the entry, computed in
> the manner prescribed;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(d) the identity and official designation of the person or system
> by which, or on whose behalf, the entry was made; and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(e) the time at which the entry was made.
>
> (3) **An entry in the Ledger shall not be altered, deleted, or replaced.**
> Where an error requires correction, a fresh entry recording the correction
> shall be appended and shall identify the entry it supersedes and the reason
> for the correction, approved by an officer not below the rank of Tehsildar.
>
> (4) The Ledger is a record of the contents and history of the land record.
> **It is not, and shall not be construed as, a record of title**, which
> continues to be determined by the record-of-rights and the orders of
> competent revenue authorities.
>
> **Explanation.** The purpose of this section is to enable proof of what the
> land record stated at a particular time and whether it has since been
> altered; it is not to alter any substantive right in land.

**Drafting notes:**

* Sub-section (3) is the provision that makes `db/migrations/002_append_only.sql`
  legally operative rather than merely a technical convention. Without it, the
  append-only guarantee is a matter of database administration.
* Sub-section (4) is not boilerplate. Without it, there is a real risk that a
  court treats the ledger as a competing record of rights and the whole system
  becomes a second, worse, source of title disputes.
* "in the manner prescribed" is deliberate — the hash algorithm must be
  changeable by rule without amending the Act. SHA-256 will not be the right
  answer forever.

### 2.2 Provision 2 — Admissibility and presumptions

**Intent:** make a chain-verified record usable in court without calling a
systems administrator to explain SHA-256.

> **44-B. Evidentiary status of Ledger entries and verification reports.**
>
> (1) A document purporting to be a verification report generated by the
> Integrity Ledger, and signed by the officer or system authorised in this
> behalf, shall be admissible in evidence in any proceedings before a Revenue
> Officer or Revenue Court, notwithstanding anything contained in the Qanun-e-Shahadat
> Order, 1984, **without proof of the custody, integrity, or manner of
> generation of the electronic record** from which it was produced.
>
> (2) Where a verification report certifies that the entries of the Ledger for
> a specified period are continuous and unaltered, the Court **shall presume**
> that the land record recorded in those entries was not altered during that
> period, until the contrary is proved.
>
> (3) The presumption in sub-section (2) is rebuttable. It may be rebutted by
> evidence that:
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(a) the Ledger, or the system maintaining it, was compromised; or
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(b) the entry was inaccurate when first recorded.
>
> (4) A cryptographic hash published by the Board of Revenue in the official
> Gazette, or on a website notified for the purpose, shall be **judicially
> noticed** as the hash published on the date stated therein.

**Drafting notes:**

* Sub-section (2) creates a *presumption of non-alteration*, not a presumption
  of accuracy. This distinction is the whole integrity-vs-truth point from
  [05-threat-model.md](05-threat-model.md) §3.1, and it must survive into the
  statute or the provision will be misapplied.
* Sub-section (3)(b) exists because a false entry made on day one is faithfully
  preserved by the chain. The law must say so, or a verified chain will be
  treated as proof the underlying facts are true.
* Sub-section (4) is what makes published Merkle anchors legally useful — it
  removes the need to prove the gazette is the gazette.

### 2.3 Provision 3 — Ground verification evidence

**Intent:** give a scored site visit a defined evidentiary standing, tied to
the trust score rather than to a bare assertion of verification.

> **44-C. Ground verification.**
>
> (1) Where a mutation relates to a sale, gift, exchange, or partition, the
> Revenue Officer shall not attest the mutation unless a ground verification
> has been recorded in the prescribed manner by a revenue officer who attended
> upon the land.
>
> (2) A ground verification record shall comprise the location of the land as
> determined at the time of attendance, a photograph taken at that time by the
> device used for the purpose, and the identification of not fewer than two
> persons residing in the vicinity who attest to the possession of the land.
>
> (3) A ground verification record shall state the **trust grade** assigned to
> it by the prescribed system, being a measure of the reliability of the
> record derived from the circumstances of its capture.
>
> (4) A ground verification record shall be admissible as evidence of the
> matters recorded therein. **The weight to be attached to it shall be
> determined having regard to the trust grade stated under sub-section (3),**
> and a record assigned the lowest grade shall not, by itself, be sufficient
> to prove possession.

**Drafting notes:**

* Sub-section (3) is unusual and deliberate. Rather than the law pretending
  that all site visits are equal, it puts the trust score on the face of the
  record and lets the court weigh accordingly. See
  [05-threat-model.md](05-threat-model.md) §3.3 for why a single visit cannot
  be treated as conclusive.
* Sub-section (2) requires two witnesses from the vicinity. The identity
  numbers of those witnesses must be recorded only in hashed form — the rules
  under this section should say so explicitly, or an over-literal implementing
  officer will build a CNIC database.
* The "prescribed manner" must not specify a particular application or vendor.
  It should specify outcomes (contemporaneous location, contemporaneous
  photograph, two local witnesses) so that the technology can change.

### 2.4 Provision 4 — Offences

**Intent:** tampering with the integrity layer, and procuring false ground
evidence, must be distinct offences — not prosecuted as generic forgery.

> **Amend section 66 (or insert 66-A):**

> **66-A. Offences relating to the Integrity Ledger.**
>
> (1) Whoever, being entrusted with the custody or administration of the
> Integrity Ledger or any part thereof, **dishonestly or fraudulently alters,
> destroys, removes, or suppresses any entry** therein, or dishonestly causes
> any entry to be recorded therein which he knows to be false, shall be
> punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend
> to seven years, and shall also be liable to fine.
>
> (2) Whoever, being a revenue officer, **records or procures the recording of
> a ground verification without having attended upon the land**, or procures
> another to do so, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description
> for a term which may extend to three years, and shall also be liable to
> fine.
>
> (3) Whoever, not being a revenue officer, **abets** the commission of an
> offence under sub-section (1) or (2), or **procures a false ground
> verification by impersonating** a person residing in the vicinity, shall be
> punished as if he had committed the offence.
>
> (4) An offence under this section shall be **non-compoundable** and shall be
> tried by a Court of Session.

**Drafting notes:**

* Sub-section (1) covers the realistic offender: not a hacker, but an insider
  with DDL rights who drops the append-only trigger, edits a number, and puts
  the trigger back. That scenario is demonstrated in `scripts/demo.py` scene 5.
* Sub-section (2) is the "office verification" offence — filing a site visit
  from the tehsil office. It needs to be a specific offence because generic
  forgery provisions require proving intent to cause wrongful loss, which is
  hard when the visit form is otherwise complete.
* Sub-section (4): non-compoundable. The whole point of an anti-corruption
  provision is defeated if it can be settled privately.

## 3. Transitional provisions

> **3.1 Continued validity of existing records.** All mutations and Fards
> attested before the commencement of this Act shall continue to be valid
> notwithstanding that no entry relating to them exists in the Integrity
> Ledger.
>
> **3.2 No suspension of existing process.** The coming into force of this Act
> shall not operate to suspend, repeal, or render invalid any manual or
> electronic process for the attestation of mutations or the issuance of Fards
> in force immediately before such commencement.
>
> **3.3 Phased application.** The Board of Revenue may, by notification,
> apply the provisions of this Act to such districts and to such classes of
> transactions as may be specified, and different dates may be appointed for
> different districts.

**Why 3.2 matters more than anything else in this document.** Punjab used
rule 36-A of the PLRA Rules 1968 to suspend Fard issuance so that the Green
Property Certificate would be the sole valid instrument
[9](https://www.dawn.com/news/1995916); within ten days of province-wide
mandate, Fard issuance had to be restored in non-digitised areas
[5](https://propakistani.pk/2026/07/11/punjab-restores-manual-land-record-certificates/),
and the scheme was suspended for six months in August 2026 [1](https://propakistani.pk/2026/08/28/punjab-suspends-digital-land-record-certificates-for-6-months/).

Clause 3.2 writes the opposite rule into the statute: **the new system adds; it
does not take away.** This is a deliberate, structural rejection of the Punjab
approach, and it should be defended in the Statement of Objects and Reasons
rather than buried in the transitional clauses.

## 4. Rules to be made under the Act

| Subject | Provision | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hash algorithm and canonical form | 44-A(2)(c) | Must be changeable by rule; specify SHA-256 initially |
| Anchoring interval and publication venue | 44-A | Gazette + notified website; quarterly minimum |
| Authorised signatories for verification reports | 44-B(1) | Two-person rule recommended |
| Trust grade definitions and thresholds | 44-C(3) | Mirror `backend/app/core/ground_trust.py` bands |
| Retention of hashed witness identifiers | 44-C(2) | **Mandate hashing; prohibit CNIC storage** |
| Correction procedure | 44-A(3) | Written reason, approver not below Tehsildar |

## 5. Legislative checklist

- [ ] Law Department vetting
- [ ] Board of Revenue approval of the technical schedule (hash algorithm,
      trust grades, anchoring interval)
- [ ] KP Assembly standing committee briefing, including the Punjab
      comparison — **present the failure analysis honestly**; if the committee
      hears it from the opposition six months later, the amendment stalls
- [ ] Statement of Objects and Reasons to state explicitly that the manual
      register is **not** suspended
- [ ] Gazette notification of the first applying districts (Phase 2 districts,
      not all fourteen)
- [ ] Rules framed within 90 days of assent
- [ ] Revenue judiciary orientation before the first case reaches a court

## 6. What to say to a legislator who asks "why not just fix the record?"

Because the record cannot be fixed by statute. The patwari association's
account of Punjab applies equally to KP: no comprehensive settlement survey in
125 years, data-entry errors from 2007 still propagating, indivisible records,
multiple properties under a single survey number, outdated boundary
demarcation [11](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2620479).

This Act does not fix those. It makes it possible to tell, in one afternoon,
whether a disputed entry was **tampered with** or whether it has **always been
wrong** — and those two facts have completely different remedies. Today,
neither can be established, which is why a minister cannot answer a
constituent's complaint and why the blame lands on whoever last touched the
system.
